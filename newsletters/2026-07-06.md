## 🔬 Deep dive — Pickens + CeeDee: a 1-2 punch, or do they eat each other?

You said you'd only draft the Pickens+Lamb combo if convinced they're a *real* 1-2, not competing
for the same targets. Good news and a correction: **we don't have to project this — it already
happened.** Pickens was traded to Dallas in **May 2025**, so 2026 is Year 2 of the pairing, and
they played a full season side by side. (Which also means your mock phrasing — "CeeDee *without*
George Pickens" — has it backwards; they're teammates.)

**What one shared season showed (A):**

| Player | Targets (share) | Rec/Yds/TD | G | 2025 finish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pickens | 137 (23%) | 93 / 1,429 / **9** | 17 | **WR5 PPR** — ~34% red-zone target share |
| CeeDee | 117 | 75 / 1,077 / **3** (career-low TD) | 14 (missed 3, ankle) | **WR19 half-PPR / WR15 per-game** |

Our own pipeline agrees on the ordering: **Pickens 14.4 PPG vs. Lamb 12.6** under our scoring. This
is a **Chase/Higgins-style 1a/1b hierarchy**, not two co-alphas. Pickens took the plurality of the
red-zone and explosive-play work (9 TD); Lamb's scoring cratered even as his target share stayed
respectable. **The honest confound:** three of Lamb's missed games fed Pickens' counting stats, so
treat 2025 as *suggestive, not a clean both-healthy test* — I'm not claiming causation off one
injury-shortened season.

**Why it matters in OUR format specifically:** 6-pt passing TDs and the +2 bonus on 40+ yard plays
reward exactly what Pickens does — win the end zone and the deep ball. The comps back the shape:
Chase/Higgins '25 (clear 1a/1b, WR2 is TD-efficiency-dependent on lower volume) fits; the bullish
ceiling is St. Brown/Williams '25 (role/aDOT-separated tandem, both weekly-relevant) but that needs
cleaner separation than Dallas has shown. And Dallas's own 2020-21 Cooper/Gallup/Lamb room is an
in-house warning about target fragmentation.

**Verdict:** both are startable in a 10-team league — but **not the equal 1-2 you're picturing.**
Draft *one*; you don't need the other. If ranking, **Pickens first** (locked RZ role, needs a lower
target share to pay off, tag security), with Lamb a high-floor complementary WR whose price should
already reflect this discount (the market's applied it). **Failure mode for the bull case:** big team
volume didn't save Lamb's TDs in '25; Schottenheimer (Year 2, still calls plays) could rebalance
toward Lamb and squeeze Pickens instead; and a Dak dip or heavier Javonte usage shrinks the pie for
both at once. Full writeup added to `research/coach-tendencies.md` (new Dallas entry).
