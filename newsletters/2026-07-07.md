## 🔬 Deep dive — Does "you can always scrounge a WR, but not a RB" actually hold? (Lane A, real data)

Your mock-memo thesis, quantified against nine years of nflverse data under *this league's exact
scoring* (`pipeline/positional_retention.py`, new tonight). The answer is a genuine split — it
vindicates the *logic* of your build while demolishing the *strong form* of the slogan.

**The apples-to-apples cut — matched-depth retention [S] (retention = % of this year's tier
still in-tier next year):**

| Pos | Tier | Retention | Survival (played ≥6 games) | PPG drop-off among returners |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RB | top-12 | **49.1%** | 90.7% | 2.37 |
| WR | top-12 | **48.1%** | 93.5% | 1.78 |
| RB | top-24 | 57.9% | 90.3% | 1.88 |
| WR | top-24 | 57.9% | 95.4% | 1.53 |

**What dies:** "elite WRs are safe, elite RBs are landmines." At matched depth, retention is a
near-coin-flip and **statistically identical** (49.1% vs 48.1% at top-12; 57.9% both at top-24;
N=108-216 per cell, 9 season-pairs). At *your pick 4*, an elite RB is not meaningfully more
likely to bust than an elite WR. Full stop.

**What survives — the mechanism behind your instinct:** RBs (a) **survive at a lower rate** (miss
more games / lose jobs — 90.7% vs 93.5% at the top), and (b) **decline harder when they do come
back** (drop-off 2.37 vs 1.78). And the WR *pool* is deeper and more survivable — top-24 WRs
survive at 95.4% with only a 1.53 PPG drop-off. That last number *is* "you can always scrounge a
WR": startable receivers keep reappearing, so the replacement cost of a WR miss is low. The
replacement cost of an RB miss is high. **Net: the RB-scarcity edge for drafting RB early is
real but modest (attrition + steeper decline); the "WR is replaceable" half of your mantra is
the better-supported half.** Which is exactly why the *build* (let the RB run happen, scoop
falling WR value, stay deep at the replaceable position) is sound — even though "top RBs are
fragile" is not the reason.

**Bonus, because you start a flex-IDP:** elite IDP is the *least* sticky tier in the whole study
— top-12 IDP retention is **10.2%** [S]. Last year's IDP stud is essentially noise, because
top-12 IDP scoring is driven by non-repeating big plays (sack 4, INT 6, FF 3, PD 3). But a
*floor* is predictable: selecting by prior-year IDP PPG hits next-year top-36 at 23.1% vs 5.5%
random [S]. **Actionable: treat the flex-IDP as a weekly streaming/matchup slot, not a
draft-and-hold — anchor on high-volume every-down LBs for the solo-tackle floor, rank by last
year's total IDP PPG, and stream the ceiling.**

*(Caveat, kept honest: Ns are modest — 9 season-pairs — so treat single-point gaps as ties, and
PPG here under-counts the 40+ yд bonuses, which if anything understates RB volatility. Full
