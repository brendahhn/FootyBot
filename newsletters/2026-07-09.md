## 🏈 Headline — We tested "RBs are overvalued here, just scrounge a WR" on 7 years of *your* league. It doesn't hold.

Your founding instinct — *"running backs get so much more valued in my league… you can always
scrounge a receiver, but not a running back"* — is the premise your whole build rests on. Tonight
Lane A finally put it on trial with the real data: every manager's **opening build** (the position
shape of their first three picks) from `draft_history_enriched.csv`, joined to where they actually
**finished** (`league_finishes.csv`), 60 manager-seasons, 2019-2023 + 2025 (new script
`pipeline/draft_builds.py`).

**The verdict is mostly a wash — but the only lean in the data runs *opposite* to your premise [S]:**

| Opening build (first 3 picks) | N | mean finish (1=best) | % top-3 | % title |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RB-heavy (2+ RB) | 29 | **5.24** | 41% | 10% |
| Hero-RB (1 RB) | 26 | 5.65 | 23% | 12% |
| **Zero-RB (0 RB in first 3)** | 5 | **6.20** | **0%** | **0%** |

Order-sensitive, the cleanest cell is **RB-RB to open: N=18, mean finish 4.67, 50% top-3, 17%
titles — the best on record** [S]. Meanwhile the shape that's supposed to be the smart-money play
in a "RB-overvalued" room — punting RB, loading WR — has the **worst mean finish and has never
produced a top-3 team in your league's history.** If RBs were genuinely overpriced here, WR-first
and Zero-RB rosters should be lapping the field on the value they're banking. They aren't.

**Now the honest part, because this is exactly where a lazy version would oversell.** That RB-RB
edge (~0.8 places) sits **inside the noise band.** With 6 seasons and 10 managers, each cell is a
handful of teams, and we already know from Finding 6 that draft-value-at-the-pick explains only
r≈+0.31 of final rank while in-season management and schedule luck move it independently. So the
RB-RB cell's success is *just as plausibly* "the managers who nailed their picks happened to open
RB-RB." **The data cannot separate build shape from pick quality — and pick quality is the bigger
lever.** Do not read this as "always draft RB-RB."

**What it does say, actionably:** your read that *elite WR value falls to you at 4* is still true
and still your edge (9 of 10 here draft RB-first — take the WR that slides). But the *second* half
of the slogan — "just scrounge the RB later" — is the part with no support and a little evidence
against it. The one build that never podiums is the one where you punt RB entirely. And your own
history is the tell: your **single best finish (3rd, 2021) was your only true RB-RB open**, and
your **worst-scoring team ever (WR-WR-WR, 2023, 1,370 PF)** was the Zero-RB dream in practice [S,
N=1 each — anecdote, not proof]. Translation for Aug 28: **take the elite WR at 4, then actually
secure a real RB at 17 — don't punt the position hoping to scrounge.** Shape is a weak tiebreaker;
who you pick is the game.

## 🔬 Deep dive — The Arizona volume mirage, and the one player you should NOT fade for "volume regression"

Your note — *"Jacoby Brissett passed at an all-time-high rate in 2025, will regress if/when he
plays"* — sent Lane D down a rabbit hole that turned into a general **draft rule**: separate players
whose 2025 numbers came from a *player's own role* from players who merely *rode an unsustainable
passing environment*. The first kind repeats; the second kind evaporates. New file:
`research/qb-environment.md`.

**The Arizona environment was a genuine, computed outlier [S].** The 2025 Cardinals **led the entire
NFL in pass attempts (649) and pass rate (63.9%)** and finished **dead last in rush attempts (366).**
Their own recent pass-rate history: 54.4% / 60.5% / 54.1% / 54.0% in 2021-24 → **63.9% in 2025**, a
~10-point franchise spike. It wasn't an identity; it was three one-year shocks stacked: Brissett
went **1-11** starting for an injured Kyler Murray, the defense bled **35.2 pts/game over the final
nine (last in NFL)** so they trailed constantly, **RB1 James Conner played 3 games**, and MHJ missed
time. That's a garbage-time pass-volume machine, and it **regresses** — Conner returns, MHJ gets
healthy, and even a mediocre defense normalizes the game script. (Your "will regress if he sits" is
now stale, by the way: Murray was released and **Brissett is the projected 2026 starter** — so the
regression comes from the *run game and defense*, not from Brissett benched.)

**Who was riding it (fade the counting stats) [A]:**
- **Trey McBride — a modest fade, still TE1.** His monster 2025 (**169 targets, 126-1,239-11, 18.6
  PPG**) leaned on NFL-leading team volume + injured target competition. The talent and role are
  real — he's still a top-2 TE and a target hog at TE is a cheat code in your scoring — but **don't
  pay as if 169 targets and 315 PPR repeat.** Draft him a touch later than his 2025 overall finish.
  *(This also explains the crater on our own board — McBride was our biggest half-PPR TE faller; now
  we know the mechanism, not just the haircut.)*
- **Michael Wilson — the cleanest environment-rider on the board; actively avoid at his 2025 price.**
  126 targets / 78-1,006-7 required *both* league-leading volume *and* everyone around him hurt. He's
  a WR4/5 in reality, not the WR2 he looked like down the 2025 stretch.
- **Marvin Harrison Jr — a QB-capped bounce-back, not a league-winner.** Health + a Year-3 leap is
  the up-case, but pass volume falls *and* the QB is Brissett/Beck. Neutral-to-slightly-up; don't
  pay for the ceiling.

**The anti-fade — this is the part that actually saves you a pick 4 mistake [A].** The same logic
looks like it should torch **Ja'Marr Chase**: the 2025 Bengals ranked **#2 in the NFL in pass
attempts (640)** — and that was **Burrow-injury-driven** (Grade-3 turf toe, **9 games missed**,
backups chucking while trailing). The naive read: "Bengals volume regresses when Burrow's healthy →
fade Chase." **That's exactly backwards.** Chase posted **185 targets, 125-1,412-8 with Burrow out
half the season** — his volume is *his own role*, not the environment. Burrow's return **upgrades
efficiency and TD rate**; even if raw team attempts fall from 640 toward the mid-500s, Chase's
per-target quality rises to offset, and in a −2-turnover / +2-big-play league his profile is ideal.
**Do not fade Chase for "volume regression" — he's a locked first-rounder, and if he's the elite WR
that falls to you at 4, the environment noise is a reason to take him, not to flinch.** His floor is
absurd: the disaster case (Burrow hurt) *already happened in 2025* and he was still an elite WR1.

**Two more the same lens flags [A/B]:** **DeVonta Smith UP** (inherits A.J. Brown's ~121 vacated
Eagles targets, QB continuity with Hurts — magnitude only moderate because Philly is the most
run-heavy team in football, so the *share* rises but the *pie* is small), and **New England's
incumbent pass-catchers DOWN** (Doubs, Hunter Henry — Brown concentrates ~25%+ of the targets). The
one-line rule for your board: **volume from a bad team trailing + injuries regresses; volume from a
player's own role that survived a QB injury does not.**
