## 🔬 Deep dive

### 4a. Is elite RB really more "volatile" than WR? (the data says no — S-tier)

You've said it twice now: *"you can always scrounge a receiver, but not a running back."* We tested it directly against 10 years of league-scored nflverse data (`pipeline/positional_stability.py`, REG only, ≥6 games/season, pooled 2016→2025). Result: **you're half right, and the wrong half is the one people repeat.**

| | top-12 retention (N→N+1) | top-24 retention | YoY PPG correlation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RB** | **47.2%** | 55.6% | r = 0.682 |
| **WR** | 39.8% | 53.7% | r = 0.702 |
| **TE** | 41.7% | 59.3% | r = 0.712 |

- **"RBs are volatile / WRs are stable" — FALSE.** The elite RB tier actually *holds its members more often* than the elite WR tier (47% vs 40% at top-12), and the year-over-year correlations are equal within noise. Elite RB production is not less stable than elite WR production. (If anything this *understates* RB stability — a top-12 RB lost mid-season to injury counts as "not retained.")
- **"You can scrounge a WR" — TRUE, but for a different reason than you think.** It's not stability, it's **supply**: the qualifying WR pool runs ~150–188/season vs ~95–106 RBs — the WR replacement pool is consistently ~50% deeper.

**Why this matters for pick 4:** the case for taking the elite WR is NOT "RBs are replaceable/volatile" (they aren't — elite RB is scarce *and* sticky). It's narrower and sharper: WR *supply depth* means you can wait on the position, so an early pick is best spent on a scarce, sticky elite RB — *when it's the right one.* The disqualifier for CMC is player-specific (age 30, 450 touches), not positional. So this deep dive and the headline don't contradict — they compound: value the elite RB tier, just not *this* 30-year-old one, especially when a WR value is falling to you anyway.
