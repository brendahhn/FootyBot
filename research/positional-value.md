# 2025 positional value under this league's scoring (S-tier: pipeline-computed)

Generated by `pipeline/positional_value.py` from real nflverse weekly data
(`data/raw/player_stats_weekly.csv`), scored with this league's exact formula
(`pipeline/league_scoring.py`). All numbers below are pipeline output, not
hand-entered.

## Method

- Scope: **2025 regular season only** (`season_type == REG`).
- For each player, sum per-week league points into a season total, divide by
  games played for **points per game (PPG)**.
- Rank players within each position by PPG.
- **Min-games filter: >= 8 games.** This removes injury-shortened and
  cup-of-coffee lines that would otherwise let a backup with one huge week top
  the per-game list. (Verified: no such artifact survives the filter.)
- Tiers reported are 1-indexed positional ranks by PPG.

**Known gap (PPG slightly under-counts):** `league_scoring.offense_points`
cannot see the +2 bonus for 40+ yard runs/receptions or offensive
fumble-return TDs from weekly aggregate data. Every PPG below undercounts real
league points by a small, boom-play-dependent amount. This affects big-play
players (deep-threat WRs, home-run RBs) slightly more than possession types.

## Read

- **QB edge (waiting on QB):** Even in this 6pt-passing / 25-yds-per-point
  league, the elite-vs-streamer QB gap is only ~3.0 PPG (~51 pts/season). QB is
  deep — 24 QBs cleared ~16.5 PPG and the QB1-to-QB12 drop is under 5 PPG.
  Waiting and striking a mid-tier starter in rounds 5-6 costs little; the edge
  does not justify a premium pick.
- **RB vs WR at pick #4:** RBs are scarcer at the top. RB1-6 (21.5 -> 16.8, a
  4.7 drop by RB6) fall off faster than WR1-6 (19.4 -> 14.4) in absolute PPG,
  and elite RB PPG tops elite WR PPG. At pick 4 the highest-value, hardest-to-
  replace asset is a top-5 RB (the CMC/Taylor/Bijan/Gibbs tier); WR depth
  extends much further (162 qualified vs 97 RB), so WR can be filled later.
