# QB-Environment Regression Layer — 2026 Draft Edge

**Lane D (Rabbit Hole) — compiled 2026-07-09.** League context: 10-team, 0.5-PPR, **6-pt passing TD**, -2 turnovers, +2 for 40+yd plays, 1 flex-IDP. Brendan drafts 4th of 10.

**Thesis:** some 2025 skill-player lines were inflated or deflated by an unsustainable team pass-rate / QB environment. Separating players who ride an environment from players who own their role is a draft edge. Tiers: **S** = computed from `data/raw/player_stats_weekly.csv`; **A/B** = web-sourced, named.

## THREAD 1 — Jacoby Brissett & the Arizona Cardinals (the anchor case)

### The environment (tier S — computed)
- **Team:** Arizona Cardinals. Brissett signed a 2-yr/$12.5M deal in March 2025, replaced an **injured Kyler Murray** (ankle/foot) starting **Week 6**, and started **14 games**, going **1-11** as a starter. (ESPN/NFL.com/PFN)
- **The volume was genuinely elite/outlier:** ARI led the **entire NFL in 2025 in both pass attempts (649) and pass rate (63.9%)**, while finishing **last in the NFL in rush attempts (366)**.
  - ARI pass-rate history (computed): 2021 **54.4%**, 2022 60.5%, 2023 **54.1%**, 2024 **54.0%**, **2025 63.9%.** The 2025 number is ~10 points above their own recent baseline — a clear franchise outlier, not a stable identity.
- **Why it's unsustainable — three compounding one-year shocks:**
  1. **Game script:** 1-11 as a starter, and the defense allowed **35.2 pts/game over the final nine games (last in NFL)** → constant trailing → garbage-time pass volume.
  2. **Run game gutted:** RB1 **James Conner played only 3 games** (season-ending injury), removing the run identity and force-feeding the pass.
  3. **WR room decimated:** Marvin Harrison Jr. missed time (12 games), funneling targets to whoever was healthy.

**Verdict on Brendan's note:** the "all-time-high pass rate" instinct is directionally right — it was **NFL-leading and a franchise-recent high driven by game script**, though not literally an NFL record. **But the note's framing "will regress if/when he plays" is now stale:** Murray was **released** (post-June-1; Vikings the favorite to sign him), and **Brissett is Arizona's projected 2026 Week 1 starter** (young Carson Beck is the alternative). So Brissett plays — the regression does **not** come from him sitting. It comes from **(a) Conner returning, (b) MHJ healthy reclaiming targets, (c) game-script normalization** if the new staff (HC Mike LaFleur / OC Nathaniel Hackett) fields even a mediocre defense. The 649 attempts almost certainly fall toward the low-to-mid 500s.

### Players affected

**Trey McBride (TE, age 26 in 2026) — SMALL/MODERATE DOWNGRADE on counting stats, still TE1. Tier A.**
- 2025 (computed): **169 targets, 126-1,239-11, 315.9 PPR (18.6/g)** — led all TEs by a wide margin.
- **Mechanism:** ~169 targets is a top-of-league share that leaned on NFL-leading team volume + injured target competition. With Conner back, MHJ healthy, and volume regressing, the raw target/PPR ceiling comes down.
- **Direction:** down on counting stats, but the **talent and role are real** — he's still a top-2 TE. In a 6-pt-pass-TD, 0.5-PPR build a target hog at TE is a positional cheat code. Treat as a modest fade off his 2025 overall finish, **not** an avoid. (Footballguys aptly titled him "Elite Player, Egregious Environment.")
- **Failure mode:** if the Cardinals are bad again (new QB struggles + bad defense) they trail again → volume stays elevated and McBride repeats. Also LaFleur/Hackett could scheme him heavily regardless. If you're wrong, you're wrong by drafting him a round too late — a soft error.

**Michael Wilson (WR, age 26 in 2026) — CLEAR DOWNGRADE. Tier A/B.**
- 2025 (computed): **126 targets, 78-1,006-7, 220.6 PPR.** Was the overall fantasy WR2 in Weeks 11-18.
- **Mechanism:** the purest environment-rider on the board. His 126 targets required *both* NFL-leading team pass volume *and* MHJ/others being hurt. With MHJ healthy, Conner back, a run-game rebalance, and likely a QB downgrade, his target share and volume both shrink.
- **Direction:** down, meaningfully. Draft him as a WR4/5 dart, not off his 2025 WR2 stretch.
- **Failure mode:** if MHJ busts/gets hurt *again* and the team is bad again, Wilson re-inherits WR1 volume. But betting on a repeat of that exact injury luck is the low-probability side.

**Marvin Harrison Jr. (WR, age 24 in 2026) — mild UPGRADE candidate, capped by the QB. Tier B.**
- 2025 (computed): only **12 games, 73 tgt, 41-608-4** — disappointing/injury-shortened.
- **Mechanism:** health + Year-3 leap + reclaimed target share is the up-case. But the environment cuts against him: pass volume regresses *and* the QB is Brissett/Beck rather than an ascending passer. Net: a bounce-back bet whose ceiling is taxed by the QB. Neutral-to-slightly-up; don't pay for a league-winner ceiling.
- **Failure mode:** QB play craters the whole passing game, or the target tree stays split three ways (McBride/Wilson/MHJ).

## THREAD 2 — Systematized QB / pass-rate up- & down-grades for 2026

### 1. DeVonta Smith (WR, PHI, age 27 in 2026) — UPGRADE. Tier A.
- **Mechanism (target vacuum):** the Eagles traded **A.J. Brown to New England**. Brown's 2025 Philly line (computed): **121 tgt, 78-1,003-7 in 15 games** now vacates. Smith (2025 computed: **113 tgt, 77-1,008-4**) becomes the **unquestioned WR1** and inherits the alpha's targets and downfield air yards. QB continuity (Jalen Hurts stays) means no passer-quality risk.
- **Direction:** up — more targets, more air yards, WR1 route priority. Nudge him up your board.
- **Failure mode / why it's moderate not huge:** the Eagles are the **most run-heavy team in football** (Saquon Barkley; low team pass volume) — the target *share* rises but the *pie* is small, capping the ceiling. A new WR2/rookie or the TE could also soak some vacated looks. Direction confidence high; magnitude moderate. Note the 4-TD 2025 is low for his yardage — positive TD regression is a second tailwind.

### 2. A.J. Brown (WR, NE, age 29 in 2026) + Drake Maye — UPGRADE (both). Tier B.
- **Mechanism (QB/role change):** Brown leaves a run-heavy, low-volume Philly attack for New England as the clear WR1 for **ascending Drake Maye (age 24)**, who in 2025 was **QB2 in fantasy PPG** and led the NFL in completion rate (72%) and yards per attempt (8.9). Patriots ~502 pass attempts in 2025 with room to rise. Brown finally pairs elite target share with a rising passer.
- **Direction:** Brown's *volume* likely rises vs. Philly's committee; Maye gets a true alpha and should hold/extend his QB1-caliber scoring — relevant in a **6-pt-pass-TD** league where a rising-volume young QB is exactly what you want.
- **Failure mode:** Brown is 29 and missed two games in 2025; age/injury is live. NE's OL/scheme is unproven and could stay run-leaning, or Maye could regress from an efficient-but-lowish-volume 2025 toward the mean. Tier B because the environment is new and unproven on both sides.

### 3. New England incumbent pass-catchers (Romeo Doubs, Hunter Henry, et al.) — DOWNGRADE. Tier B.
- **Mechanism (target concentration):** Brown is projected to command ~25%+ of NE targets (~130). That target share comes from somewhere — the incumbents (Doubs, Henry, Boutte) each lose looks. ESPN/CBS/NBC all flagged Doubs specifically as squeezed out of a top-30 target finish.
- **Direction:** down — fade the NE secondary pass-catchers off any 2025 baseline.
- **Failure mode:** if Maye's *total* volume rises enough, the pie grows and absolute targets hold even at a lower share; or one incumbent consolidates the clear #2 role and is fine. It's a share-vs-volume tug-of-war, hence tier B.

### 4. Ja'Marr Chase (WR, CIN, age 26 in 2026) — SAFE / slight UPGRADE (anti-fade flag). Tier A.
- **This is the flip side of the thesis — a player whose 2025 looks environment-boosted but is actually pure own-role.** The Bengals ranked **#2 in the NFL in pass attempts (640, computed)** in 2025 — but that volume was **game-script/backup-driven**: **Joe Burrow missed 9 games (Grade 3 turf toe, Week 2)**, backups chucked while the team trailed. The naive read is "Bengals volume regresses with Burrow healthy → fade Chase."
- **Why that's wrong:** Chase posted **185 targets, 125-1,412-8** and stayed elite *even with Burrow out half the year*. His volume is his own role, not the environment. Burrow's return **upgrades efficiency and TD rate**; even if raw team attempts fall from 640 toward the mid-500s as game script normalizes, Chase's per-target quality rises to offset. In a **-2-turnover, +2-for-40+yd** league his big-play/low-mistake profile is ideal.
- **Direction:** net flat-to-up; **do not fade him for "volume regression."** Locked first-rounder.
- **Failure mode:** Burrow re-injures the toe — but even that scenario *already happened in 2025* and Chase still finished as an elite WR1, so his floor is unusually high. The only real down-case is a Chase injury of his own.

### Non-movers checked and dismissed (so you don't chase a phantom edge)
- **George Pickens (DAL):** the "Pickens = Cowboys" note is **continuity, not a 2026 move** — the data shows him on Dallas for all of 2025 (**1,429 rec yds, 9 TD** with Dak Prescott). No QB change; no environment shift. His 2025 was real. (Two stray Week-16/18 "KC" zero rows in the CSV are a data artifact — ignore.)

## HOW TO USE THIS AT THE DRAFT (drafting 4th of 10)

1. **Fade the Arizona volume mirage.** McBride is still TE1 — take him confidently but expect his *counting stats* to come down; don't reach as if 315 PPR repeats. **Actively avoid Michael Wilson at his 2025 price** — he's the cleanest environment-rider on the board and a WR4/5 in reality. Treat MHJ as a capped bounce-back, not a league-winner.
2. **Buy the target vacuum: DeVonta Smith** ticks up as Philly's unquestioned WR1 — solid WR2 with a raised floor.
3. **Do NOT fade Ja'Marr Chase** on "Bengals volume regression" logic — he owns his role; Burrow's return is a tailwind, not a threat. First-round lock.
4. **Young rising QB = 6-pt-pass-TD gold.** Drake Maye (with A.J. Brown now his alpha) is a target if you punt QB — exactly the ascending-volume passer this scoring rewards. Brown himself is a fine WR2/3 with upside but tier-B risk (age 29, unproven NE offense).
5. **Fade New England's secondary pass-catchers** (Doubs, Henry) — Brown concentrates the targets.

**One-line rule of thumb:** *volume that came from a bad team trailing + injuries (Arizona's WRs) regresses; volume that came from a player's own role and survived a QB injury (Chase) does not.*
