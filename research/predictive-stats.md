# Predictive stats — year-over-year correlation with next season's PPG

Generated by `pipeline/predictive_stats.py` from real nflverse data (see `data/raw/fetch_summary.json` for exact row counts/seasons). Minimum 6 games in both seasons required per player-season pair to reduce small-sample noise. Correlation = Pearson r between the stat in season N and points-per-game in season N+1, under this league's exact scoring formula (`pipeline/league_scoring.py`), pooled across all available season pairs.

**Known gap:** league scoring's 40+ yard run/reception bonuses and offensive fumble return TDs are NOT included (not computable from weekly aggregate data) -- every PPG figure below under-counts real league points by a small, boom-play-dependent amount.

**Not computed (data not yet available):** snap% (offense + IDP), red zone touches, yards per route run. These need `snap_counts.csv`, play-by-play red-zone splits, and participation data respectively -- none provided yet.

## Positional retention — does "you can always scrounge a WR, not a RB" hold?

Generated by `pipeline/positional_retention.py` (stdlib/`csv` only) from the same nflverse weekly files under this league's exact scoring. REG season only; a player-season needs >=6 games to be ranked, and retention/drop-off are measured across each consecutive pair 2016-17 ... 2024-25 (9 pairs), then pooled. `[S]` = pipeline-computed.

Definitions: **retention** = fraction of season-N top tier that are ALSO top tier in N+1. **survival** = fraction that even played >=6 games in N+1 (i.e., didn't get hurt / lose the job / wash out). **drop-off** = tier mean PPG in N minus the same players' mean PPG in N+1 among survivors.

**Matched-depth RB vs WR (same tier size — the apples-to-apples cut) [S]:**

| Pos | Tier | N | Retention | Survival | Tier PPG (N) | Next PPG | Drop-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RB | top-12 | 108 | 49.1% | 90.7% | 16.94 | 14.57 | 2.37 |
| WR | top-12 | 108 | 48.1% | 93.5% | 15.25 | 13.47 | 1.78 |
| RB | top-24 | 216 | 57.9% | 90.3% | 14.68 | 12.80 | 1.88 |
| WR | top-24 | 216 | 57.9% | 95.4% | 13.75 | 12.21 | 1.53 |

**Read (split verdict, leans toward supporting the build logic — not the strong claim):** At matched depth, elite-RB and elite-WR *retention* is a near-coin-flip and essentially equal (top-12: RB 49.1% vs WR 48.1%; top-24: 57.9% both). So the strong form of the bet — "elite WRs are safe, elite RBs are landmines" — is **contradicted**: a top pick at either position is roughly equally likely to fall out of the tier next year, which matters at Brendan's pick 4. Where RB *is* genuinely worse, and where the scarcity thesis survives: RBs survive at a lower rate (miss more games / lose jobs — 90.7% vs 93.5% at top-12) and decline more when they do return (drop-off 2.37 vs 1.78 at top-12; 2.37 vs 1.53 at the standard tiers). And the WR *pool* is deeper and more survivable (top-24 WR 95.4% survival, only 1.53 PPG drop-off), which is the real mechanism behind "you can always scrounge a WR" — usable WRs keep appearing. Net: the scarcity edge for taking RB early is **real but modest** (injury attrition + steeper decline), while the WR-is-replaceable half of the mantra is the better-supported half.

**IDP retention (top-12 by this scoring, one flex-IDP slot) [S]:** N=108. Retention **10.2%**, survival 88.9%, tier PPG 8.06 -> next 5.53 (drop-off 2.53). Elite IDP is the *least* sticky tier in the whole study — last year's IDP stud is essentially noise, because top-12 IDP scoring is driven by big-play events (sack 4, INT 6, FF 3, PD 3) that don't repeat. Practical call: treat the flex-IDP as a weekly streaming/matchup slot, not a draft-and-hold asset.

**IDP floor — which prior-year profile to target [S]:** Pick 24 players in year N by a signal, measure hit-rate into next-year top-K IDP (N_selections=216; random baseline = K / pool size):

| Next-yr top-K | by prior-yr SOLO rate | by prior-yr IDP PPG | random baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| top-12 | 4.2% | 9.7% | 1.8% |
| top-24 | 8.8% | 17.6% | 3.7% |
| top-36 | 16.2% | 23.1% | 5.5% |

Both signals beat random ~3-4x, so a floor *is* predictable even though the ceiling isn't. This extends the r=0.506 solo-tackle finding: solo-tackle volume gives a real startable floor (16.2% into top-36 vs 5.5% random), but **full prior-year IDP PPG is the better selector** (23.1%) because it captures the solo base *plus* role/snap security. Actionable: for the flex-IDP, anchor on high-volume every-down LBs (solo floor) but rank them by last year's total IDP PPG, and stream the ceiling weekly rather than paying up for last year's sack/INT spike.

**Caveats:** (1) 2025 REG carries ~3.5x the weekly rows of prior seasons (18,539 vs ~5,300 — the fetch pulled far more fringe players for the latest year); this does not move the top-tier ranks (min-6-games elite players are unaffected) but slightly lowers the IDP random-baseline denominator for the 2024->2025 pair. (2) Same 40+ yard bonus / fumble-return-TD gap as the rest of this doc — PPG under-counts boom plays, which if anything understates RB volatility. (3) Ns are modest (9 pairs); treat single-digit percentage-point gaps (e.g. RB 49.1% vs WR 48.1% retention) as ties, not signal.
