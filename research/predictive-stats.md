# Predictive stats — year-over-year correlation with next season's PPG

Generated by `pipeline/predictive_stats.py` from real nflverse data (see `data/raw/fetch_summary.json` for exact row counts/seasons). Minimum 6 games in both seasons required per player-season pair to reduce small-sample noise. Correlation = Pearson r between the stat in season N and points-per-game in season N+1, under this league's exact scoring formula (`pipeline/league_scoring.py`), pooled across all available season pairs.

**Known gap:** league scoring's 40+ yard run/reception bonuses and offensive fumble return TDs are NOT included (not computable from weekly aggregate data) -- every PPG figure below under-counts real league points by a small, boom-play-dependent amount.

**Not computed (data not yet available):** snap% (offense + IDP), red zone touches, yards per route run. These need `snap_counts.csv`, play-by-play red-zone splits, and participation data respectively -- none provided yet.

## Positional stability — is elite RB or elite WR production stickier? (added 2026-07-03)

Generated by `pipeline/positional_stability.py` (stdlib-only, imports `league_scoring.offense_points`).
REG season only, min 6 games/season to qualify, league-scored, pooled 2016->2025. Motivated by
Brendan's stated draft premise ("you can always scrounge a receiver, but not a running back") —
tested directly. **S-tier (pipeline-computed).**

**Tier retention** (a top-N player at his position in season N, still top-N at that position in N+1):

| Position | top-12 retain | top-24 retain | N (top-12 / top-24) |
|---|---|---|---|
| RB | **47.2%** | 55.6% | 108 / 216 |
| WR | 39.8% | 53.7% | 108 / 216 |
| TE | 41.7% | **59.3%** | 108 / 216 |

**Year-over-year PPG predictability** (median Pearson r of season-N PPG -> season-N+1 PPG, players
qualifying >=6 games in BOTH seasons, 9 season-pairs): RB **0.682**, WR **0.702**, TE **0.712** —
statistically indistinguishable across positions.

**Supply depth** (qualifying players/season, >=6 games): WR ~146-188, RB ~95-106, TE ~76-100 — the
WR pool is consistently ~50% deeper than the RB pool.

**Verdict (challenges half the premise):** "RBs are volatile / WRs are stable" is FALSE on the
stability axis — the elite RB tier actually *retains* its members more often than the elite WR tier
(47.2% vs 39.8% at top-12), and the YoY PPG correlations are equal within noise. The real, verified
asymmetry is **supply**, not stability: the WR replacement pool is ~50% deeper, which is what makes
receivers "scroungeable" and elite RB genuinely scarce. So the strategic implication is the
*opposite* of the lazy read: elite RB production is BOTH scarce and sticky — spending an early pick
on the right elite RB is defensible; the disqualifier for a given RB is player-specific (age,
workload, durability), not "RBs are inherently unstable." Known caveats: injuries confound RB
"volatility" (a top-12 RB lost mid-season to injury counts as non-retained, which if anything
*understates* RB stability); `league_scoring.py` omits the +2 40-yd bonuses symmetrically across
positions. Retention measures tier persistence, not literal waiver replaceability — supply depth is
the better proxy for that.
